Neovim uses these syntax colours. Each theme's neovim.lua configures aether.nvim with Tetra's palette, editor roles and this mapping. The generator applies the same rules as this page, so new edits here reach Neovim once they're copied into it.

Palette

Every key in colors.toml. When you edit a colour, a corner patch shows its default under the current rules. Ratios are WCAG contrast against background, or for the shades, the contrast of foreground on them. Click a swatch to open the picker, or type a hex code.

Theme rules, for all three variants

Dark, after the rules below, is the definition. Each variant only sets its ground (the OKHSL lightness of its background and foreground) and an accent, which follows Dark's unless set. Every colour keeps Dark's hue and saturation. Neutrals, muted and selection sit at the same position between background and foreground. The hues keep Dark's lightness distance from the background, scaled by the light-ground depth on a light variant, so moving the foreground doesn't darken them. Normal colours are their bright colour darkened by Dark's step, held to 4.5:1. A variant is light when its background is lighter than its foreground.

Backgrounds and foregrounds become the grey with the same OKLab lightness, so the steps between them don't change.

selection sits at the hue opposite accent's and keeps its own saturation. Its lightness is a percentage of the way from black to accent's, or from white in Light (all OKHSL).

muted takes selection's OKHSL hue and saturation at its own OKHSL lightness, so it keeps its contrast as comment and line-number text.

60° steps of OKHSL hue from red, with brown in the slot after red and yellow and orange split either side of it. Each colour keeps its own OKHSL lightness and saturation.

Terminal slots

What foot and Ghostty get: ANSI 0–15, then 16–22 for tmux. Slot 22 is lighter_background mixed 3% toward foreground, for the status bar.
